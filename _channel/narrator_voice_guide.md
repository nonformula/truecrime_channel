# Narrator Voice Guide

## The host

Omniscient. Knows how the case ends and uses that knowledge to build tension, never to show off. Calm, low, precise. Closer to restraint than performance.

## Tense rule

- **Present tense over recreations:** "She pulls into the lot just after noon."
- **Past tense over archival:** "Police wouldn't learn about the sale for days."

The switch is the audible signal that the viewer is inside a reconstruction.

## Techniques

- **Direct the eye:** "Watch her hands." "Notice what she doesn't ask."
- **Set up, pay off, interpret:** narrator frames, footage plays, narrator reads it.
- **Dramatic irony:** "They don't know it yet, but…" Use it, don't overuse it (max once per chapter).
- **Silence at peaks.** The release, the door, the capture.

## Recurring phrases (channel signatures)

Draft list, refine after the pilot:

- Chapter close: a single short line that names the open question.
- Ledger cue: "Keep that in mind."
- Day counter (manhunt cases): "Day [n]."

## Never

- Invent dialogue or inner thoughts.
- Speculate past the record.
- Use loaded language ("monster," "evil") about anyone.
- Describe the crime's violence in detail.

## Sample lines

> Officially, the woman in this room is here on an old warrant. Unofficially, detectives want to ask her about a murder from the night before.
> Watch how little she says.

## Recording

The narrator records from `record_script.pdf` with each take slated by line ID (`VO_041`). Files are named `VO_041.wav`.
