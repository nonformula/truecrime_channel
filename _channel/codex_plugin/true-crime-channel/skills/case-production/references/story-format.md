# Flexible evidence-led story format

Runtime 15–30 minutes; the evidence earns the length. Functions: cold open → incident → investigation → breakthrough → pursuit → resolution. They are not mandatory chapters. Known suspect: how the case was established. Unknown suspect: discoveries narrow possibilities. Missing person: search and changing explanations. Cold case: impasse and renewed evidence. No flight: steps to arrest/trial replace pursuit. Unresolved: established findings, uncertainties and documented current status, not an invented resolution.

Choose the case's structure in its brief. Establish one investigative question and what makes each evidence connection matter. Every beat identifies its story question, what the audience learns, supporting evidence, best sound and picture, and what changes. Usually sequence setup → primary-source moment → necessary interpretation → next question; omit redundant interpretation. Meaningful developments every 30–60 seconds are a diagnostic, not a quota. Long footage earns space through unique story value. Silence can earn space through emotion. Biography is distributed, not a long early detour.

Cold open: a concise, contextualized authentic conflict and question, around 90 seconds for DARKHORSE. Do not force every case into release/flight or expose a reveal that harms the actual story. Returning to teaser material adds new context rather than repeating it.

Knowledge panel: occasional, only when helpful. Columns are “What police knew — as of [verified date/time]” and “What the audience has been shown.” Each police state needs evidence of acquisition timing, not just event timing. If acquisition time is unknown, mark it unresolved internally and omit the timing claim from the graphic. Convergence on a location at capture does not close all evidentiary questions. Do not show a panel when the chronology cannot support it.

Narrator: calm, precise, informed. Distinguish observation, allegation, inference and adjudicated finding; attribute claims and explain only what the recording/graphic cannot. No fixed VO percentage, omniscient mind-reading, behavioral-baseline analysis or gesture-based guilt cues. Tense clarifies chronology; visible labels identify recreation. Use exact checked quotes, preserve context, and let recordings breathe.

## Storytelling devices (v3.2)
The pre-script and the full script draw from this set. Combine them. A device repeated in the same shape beat after beat stops working, so vary which one carries each transition, and let some beats run on authentic sound with no device at all.

| Device | What it does | Rule of use |
|---|---|---|
| Analysis | Explains plainly what is happening and why, including investigators' decisions, strategy and lesser-known procedure | Only where the evidence supports the reading. Example: the detective knows the suspect is not under arrest and can leave, so avoids questions that signal she is being investigated |
| Audience question | Invites the viewer to weigh the evidence; carries momentum into the next beat | Arises from established facts; never implies an answer the record has not established |
| Foreshadowing | Signals that a significant discovery is approaching | Factual only. Signal that understanding will change; do not preview a conclusion the story has not earned |
| Emphasis | Vivid language at the few moments that deserve it | Words such as horrifying, shocking or disturbing are reserved for moments the evidence supports. Default register is flat and factual |
| Connect the dots | Makes a relationship between facts explicit and names what supports it | State the link and its source together. A connection without identified support is an inference and is labeled as one |
| Knowledge ledger | A short spoken recap between major beats: what investigators know, what the evidence supports, what is unresolved | A few per episode, not a fixed cadence. Grounded in the record; same two-column discipline as the knowledge panel |

Overall tone: serious, straightforward, factual. Do not speculate, and do not present an unanswered question as if it has an answer.

Ledger placement: natural slots are after the initial scene investigation, after interviews with people close to the victim, after a forensic or records result, before a major interview, and after an arrest but before trial evidence. Use only the slots the case earns.

## Picture and sound
Four picture/audio categories: authentic footage; authentic audio; cinematic recreations; investigative graphics. Plan sound independently of picture. A map can carry authentic audio. Overlapping layers occupy one sequence interval.

DARKHORSE is a pilot example, not a template every case must copy. Read `pilot-darkhorse.md` only for that case or an explicit comparison.
